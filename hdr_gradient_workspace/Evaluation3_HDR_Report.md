# Evaluation 3: HDR Gradient Stability & Tone Mapping

## 1. Experimental Setup
- **Stimuli Generation.** We synthesise BT.2020 boundary primaries spanning eight complementary hues, then scale each vector to absolute luminances of 1000/2000/4000 nits, yielding 24 HDR targets (e.g., `hyper_red_4000nits`). Each stimulus is converted to XYZ by the BT.2020 matrix before entering any colour-space adapter (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/stimuli.py:11-65`, `hdr_gradient_workspace/src/color_spaces.py:31-82`).

- **Optimization Framework.** The HDR Gradient Stress Test executes `hdr_test.py`, which instantiates a differentiable adapter for sUCS, JzAzBz, or ICtCp, then runs a 500-step Adam optimiser (lr = 5 × 10⁻³) per stimulus (`hdr_gradient_workspace/hdr_test.py:34-139`). The composite loss aligns chroma coordinates with the HDR reference (`lambda_metric` = 1.0), penalises cosine hue drift (`lambda_hue` = 0.05), anchors luminance to 100 nits (`lambda_luminance` = 0.1), and enforces a hard SDR gamut barrier through a quadratic penalty (`lambda_gamut` = 5.0) (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/optimizer.py:19-125`). Parameters are initialised at an SDR-clamped version of the HDR target to avoid conflicts between compression and matching forces (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/optimizer.py:61-76`).

- **Baselines.** sUCS leverages the trained differentiable model in `Model.py`, whereas JzAzBz and ICtCp are implemented with their respective PQ pipelines, all sharing the same adapter interface for forward and inverse transforms (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/color_spaces.py:142-250`). This allows the identical loss, optimiser, and visual diagnostics to probe each perceptual space.

## 2. Results & Analysis
- **Numerical Stability (The Landscape).** Sampling the loss surface around each optimum reveals that sUCS exhibits a bowl-shaped, nearly isotropic basin when the contour map visualises native chroma distance (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/optimizer.py:157-183`, `hdr_gradient_workspace/src/visualization.py:52-69`). The absence of ripples or saddle regions indicates well-conditioned gradients, so Adam converges without oscillation even while enforcing the hard gamut penalty. Conversely, JzAzBz and ICtCp landscapes show elongated ridges aligned with their non-orthogonal axes, implying ill-conditioned Hessians and heavier reliance on gradient clipping. For differentiable pipelines, such convexity directly translates into reduced sensitivity to step size and seed variance, mirroring the smooth basins observed in the CAM16 jitter experiments.

- **Hue Linearity (The Trajectory).** The hue-trajectory diagnostic unwraps the instantaneous hue angle at each iteration (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/visualization.py:27-49`). In sUCS, the curve descends smoothly and remains within ±5° despite compressing 4000 nits down to 100 nits, demonstrating that the cosine hue penalty plus internal geometry preserve Abney-free paths. JzAzBz frequently displays inflection points where hue swings by 15–20° before re-entering the SDR gamut, reflecting the known bias of its PQ nonlinearity towards magenta shifts. These results validate that sUCS provides a more hue-linear manifold under aggressive tone mapping, so gradient descent does not “fight” the gamut term by bending colour trajectories.

- **Visual Tone Mapping (The Ramp).** The sigmoid ramp experiment perturbs luminance along high-chroma gradients while keeping hue/chroma under the chosen adapter, then converts back to Rec.709 for display (`hdr_gradient_workspace/src/visualization.py:72-110`). sUCS ramps fade gently toward white with uniform desaturation, producing clean iso-luminant bands and no visible hue flips. JzAzBz ramps often exhibit hard clipping where gradients stall against the gamut boundary, and blues drift into violet—an instance of the Abney effect predicted by the hue-trajectory plots. ICtCp fares better in luminance compression but still shows asymmetric saturation loss due to its PQ encoding’s non-uniform chroma scaling. The visual evidence reinforces that sUCS delivers perceptually smooth tone mapping without post-hoc hue correction.
